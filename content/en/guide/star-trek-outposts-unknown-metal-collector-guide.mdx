## Why Metal Decides the Pace of Your Outpost Network

In most builder games, the flashy resource is energy or research. In *Outposts Unknown*, metal is the boring one that gates everything else. Habitats, landing pads, storage, and the machinery that keeps an outpost pressurized all trace back to how much metal you can pull out of a planet and how fast you can move it.

That creates a specific failure pattern worth watching for:

- **You overbuild one planet.** Every structure on a world consumes metal that could have seeded a second outpost.
- **You under-invest in hauling.** A rich deposit with no transport chain is a museum piece, not a supply line.
- **You ignore crew time.** Crew assigned to manual collection are crew not doing research or solving the logistical puzzles the campaign leans on.

The fix is not "collect more metal." It is "collect metal with the least crew time per unit, then spend it where it compounds." A metal collector is really a system: survey, extraction, transport, storage, refinement, and reinvestment. Break one link and the whole chain slows.

## The Metal Collection Loop, Step by Step

Every deposit you work follows the same six stages. If your metal income feels stuck, one of these stages is usually the culprit.

| Stage | What you are doing | What "good" looks like | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Survey | Scanning a planet for metal signatures | Two or more viable deposits before you commit | Settling on the first blip you see |
| 2. Claim | Choosing the outpost site | Deposit, terrain, and travel route all considered | Placing for the deposit and ignoring the route |
| 3. Extract | Assigning crew and equipment | Steady output with minimal babysitting | Manual collection that eats crew hours |
| 4. Haul | Moving ore to where it is needed | Transporter for short hops, vehicles for bulk | Hauling everything by hand |

### Survey First, Always

Scanning is cheap; relocation is expensive. Spend the scan time to confirm that a planet has enough metal density to justify a permanent structure, and check whether the surrounding terrain supports the transport method you plan to use. A deposit on a cliff edge with no clean landing approach will cost you more crew time than a poorer deposit on flat ground.

| Signal you notice | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Single isolated signature | Small or shallow deposit | Mine it with a mobile team, do not build permanent |
| Clustered signatures | Larger field, worth an outpost | Prioritize this site in your queue |
| Deposit far from a landing zone | High hauling cost | Test a transporter route before committing |
| Harsh environmental conditions | Higher upkeep, slower crew work | Build only if the metal tier justifies it |
| Deposit near a research opportunity | Double value | Site your outpost between both |

## Setting Up Your Star Trek Outposts Unknown Metal Collector

A functioning **Star Trek Outposts Unknown metal collector** is not a single building — it is a small cluster of roles that work together. Think of the outpost as a machine with four jobs: pull metal out, move it, hold it, and protect the crew doing the work.

**H3: The four roles of a healthy collection outpost**

- **Extraction:** the structures and equipment that actually pull ore from the deposit. This is the only role that must sit directly on the resource.
- **Buffer storage:** enough capacity to keep hauling efficient. If your buffer is too small, transports run half-empty; too large and you have wasted metal on silos.
- **Crew support:** whatever keeps assigned crew productive — rest, safety, and easy access between the deposit and the collection point.
- **Link back to the network:** the transporter pad, landing zone, or vehicle route that connects this outpost to your wider scientific network.

**H3: Placement checklist before you break ground**

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Is the deposit large enough to outlive the build cost? | A short-lived mine never pays back its own construction |
| Can a vehicle reach the site without a long detour? | Bulk hauling is the cheapest way to move metal |
| Is there a clear transporter line? | Fast fallback when vehicles are tied up |
| Does the terrain raise upkeep costs? | High upkeep quietly eats your metal margin |
| Does this site support another outpost nearby? | Shared logistics multiplies value |

Community reports on the demo are still thin, so the safest approach is to build your first collector small, measure output for a few cycles, and expand only after you see the numbers. Player experience with builder games of this shape consistently favors a modest outpost that runs continuously over a large one that stalls.

## Logistics: Moving Metal Without Burning Crew Time

The single biggest mistake in a **Star Trek Outposts Unknown metal collector guide** run is treating transport as an afterthought. The reference material for the game highlights crew, vehicles, and transporters as core tools for managing resources and solving logistical puzzles — which is a strong hint that moving metal is meant to be a puzzle, not a chore.

| Method | Best for | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transporter | Short hops, urgent top-ups | Instant delivery, no route needed | Ties up transporter capacity and crew attention |
| Vehicle | Bulk ore, repeatable routes | High volume per trip, low attention | Slow, terrain-dependent, needs a clear path |
| Shuttle / ship transfer | Long distance between outposts | Connects distant planets | Long cycle time, easy to mistime |
| Manual crew carry | Tiny quantities, early game | No infrastructure needed | Costs crew time you cannot spare |

A simple rule that holds up well: **transporters for time, vehicles for tonnage.** If you need metal in the next few minutes to finish a build, beam it. If you need a steady drip feeding three outposts, set up a vehicle loop and leave it alone.

Also, watch your storage placement. Metal that lands in the wrong outpost's storage is metal you have to move twice. Put your largest buffer at the site where you do the most building, not at the site that produces the most ore.

## Spending Priorities and Common Mistakes

Metal is only useful when it converts into more capacity. Spend in this order and your network compounds; spend out of order and you will be stuck re-running the same loop.

| Priority | Spend on | Payoff |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Extraction upgrades at existing outposts | Immediate income increase, no new site needed |
| 2 | One reliable transport link | Converts a stranded deposit into a real supply line |
| 3 | Modest buffer storage at your main build site | Prevents stalls during big pushes |
| 4 | A second collection outpost | Diversifies income and spreads risk |
| 5 | Comfort and cosmetic upgrades | Fine later, expensive early |

**H3: Troubleshooting a stalled metal collector**

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Metal income flat despite new outposts | Ore sitting in remote storage | Rebalance storage toward your build hub |
| Crew constantly reassigned to hauling | No automated transport route | Set a vehicle loop and stop manual carries |
| Build queue keeps stalling | Buffer too small | Add storage before adding production |
| Outpost feels like a drain | Site chosen for deposit only | Relocate to a flatter, better-connected spot |
| Research falling behind | Crew split too thin | Shrink collection scope, protect research crew |

## FAQ

**Is there a dedicated metal collector unit in Star Trek Outposts Unknown?**
Not in the sense of a single named unit you unlock. Collection is a system of extraction structures, transport, and storage. Any **Star Trek Outposts Unknown metal collector guide** worth reading will focus on that chain rather than a single building.

**How much metal should I stockpile before expanding?**
There is no fixed number, and it will change with balancing. A practical approach is to keep enough buffer to finish your current build plus one upgrade, then expand. If you are dipping into your reserve every cycle, you are expanding too fast.

**Should I use transporters or vehicles for metal hauling?**
Use transporters when speed matters and vehicles for steady bulk volume. Most players end up running both: a vehicle loop for the base load and transporters for emergency top-ups.

**Why is my metal income dropping over time?**
The most common reasons are depleted deposits, storage sitting in the wrong outpost, or crew being pulled into manual hauling. Check those three before you build anything new.

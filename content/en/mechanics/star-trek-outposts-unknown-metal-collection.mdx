Metal is the quiet bottleneck of every outpost you build in **Star Trek: Outposts Unknown**. You can have the perfect research tree mapped out and the sharpest officers in the X'Lehari System, but if your **Star Trek Outposts Unknown metal collection** stalls, everything downstream stalls with it — construction queues, module upgrades, and the story beats that depend on a functioning base. ## Why Metal Collection Decides Your Outpost's Fate

Magic Fuel Games built this as a narrative-driven outpost builder, not a pure combat game. The pitch is straightforward: construct research facilities across strange new worlds, pick your way through branching tech and building trees, and keep a fragile colony running while a mysterious cosmic force threatens the system. Every one of those systems is fed by materials.

Metal is the structural resource. It is what turns a survey marker into a landing pad, a landing pad into a research lab, and a research lab into progress. In colony sims of this type, the resource that gates construction is almost always the resource that gates your tempo — and tempo is what separates a base that survives its first crisis from one that does not.

| Resource archetype | Primary role | What stalls without it |
|---|---|---|
| Metal / alloys | Structures, modules, upgrades | Construction queues, base expansion, repairs |
| Power or energy | Keeping modules online | Production halts, research slows |
| Research data | Unlocking the tech tree | No new buildings or better extraction |
| Food and crew support | Morale and officer performance | Crew efficiency drops, tasks take longer |

*Exact resource names vary between builds — always confirm against your in-game resource panel before committing a build order.*

The practical takeaway: metal is not a "collect it when convenient" resource. It is the input that determines how fast you can respond to whatever the X'Lehari System throws at you next.

## How Metal Collection Works: The Four-Stage Loop

Metal collection in Star Trek Outposts Unknown follows a recognizable colony-sim pipeline. Understanding each stage separately matters, because a bottleneck in stage three can look exactly like a shortage in stage one — and players often fix the wrong one.

| Stage | What happens | What to watch | Best upgrade lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Survey | You locate deposits in the surrounding environment | Deposit richness and distance from base | Better scanning and exploration upgrades |
| 2. Extract | Extraction structures pull metal from a deposit | Throughput per structure, power draw | Upgrading extractors before adding new ones |
| 3. Haul | Metal moves from the deposit to storage | Travel time, crew assignment, congestion | Shorter routes and dedicated haulers |
| 4. Refine / store | Raw metal becomes usable material or sits in storage | Storage caps and processing speed | Storage modules and refineries |

The single most common misread is treating stage four as an afterthought. Storage caps silently waste extraction output. If your extractors are running and your stockpile is pinned at maximum, you are paying power and crew time for metal that never enters your economy.

### Survey Smarter, Not Wider

Exploration is a core pillar of the game — the store page leans heavily on traversing unique environments and meeting new characters. That same exploration feeds metal collection. When you push into a new area, scan before you build. A rich deposit two minutes closer to your core base is often worth more than a marginal deposit with a long haul, because hauling time compounds with every trip.

## Metal Grades and What They're Used For

Most builders in this genre sort metal into rough tiers, and Star Trek Outposts Unknown follows that logic. Treat the labels below as archetypes rather than official terminology, and check the in-game tooltips for exact names in your build.

| Tier (archetype) | Typical source | Best used for | Don't waste it on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Common structural metal | Surface deposits near your starting base | Basic modules, habitats, paths, storage | High-tier research facilities |
| Refined alloy | Processing common metal | Advanced labs, upgraded modules, officer facilities | Throwaway temporary structures |
| Rare / exotic metal | Distant or hazardous deposits | Story-critical construction, top-tier tech | Routine expansion |

The rule of thumb that saves the most grief: build your throwaway infrastructure out of common metal and hold refined and rare material for anything you cannot easily demolish. Storage is cheap; re-grinding a rare deposit is not.

## Metal Collection Priorities by Game Phase

Where you spend metal collection effort should change as your outpost matures. The table below is a general framework rather than a strict script — narrative missions will occasionally override it.

| Phase | Metal collection focus | Secondary focus | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early outpost | One strong nearby deposit, fully upgraded extractor | Basic storage so nothing is wasted | Spreading extractors across three distant sites |
| Mid-game expansion | Two to three deposits with short haul routes | Refining capacity to convert common into alloy | Overbuilding decorative or low-value modules |
| Crisis / story push | Reserve stockpile held in storage | Fast rebuild capacity after losses | Spending your entire reserve on expansion |
| Late outpost | Efficiency upgrades over new sites | Officer development for extraction tasks | Ignoring storage caps as output climbs |

Player experience across colony sims consistently shows the same pattern: the captains who struggle are rarely the ones who under-built. They are the ones who over-built early, ran out of metal mid-crisis, and had no reserve to rebuild with.

## Crew, Officers, and Logistics for Metal Collection

Your crew is not decoration. In Star Trek Outposts Unknown you assign tasks to crew and develop officers, and dangerous encounters test both morale and prowess. That means metal collection is partly a personnel problem.

- **Match officers to extraction duty.** Officers with relevant aptitudes should anchor your highest-output sites, not your most convenient ones.
- **Protect morale.** A demoralized crew works slower, and slow hauling quietly caps your entire metal intake.
- **Rotate rather than stack.** Piling every officer onto one deposit creates a single point of failure when an encounter goes badly.
- **Keep a hauler free.** A dedicated logistics assignment prevents the classic mid-game pileup where extraction outpaces delivery.

| Crew assignment | Effect on metal collection | When to prioritize |
|---|---|---|
| Extraction specialist | Higher throughput per structure | Early, when every unit counts |
| Logistics / hauler | Faster delivery, fewer idle trips | As soon as routes lengthen |
| Research officer | Unlocks better extraction tech | Before major expansion pushes |
| Generalist | Flexible, mediocre at everything | Filling gaps during crises |

## Common Metal Collection Mistakes and Fixes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Building extractors before surveying | You commit to a poor deposit for the whole run | Scan first, build second |
| Ignoring storage caps | Extraction output is silently discarded | Add storage before adding extractors |
| Upgrading breadth-first | Every module is weak; nothing is efficient | Concentrate upgrades on one site |
| Long-haul deposits early | Travel time eats your effective output | Prefer close, modest deposits first |
| No crisis reserve | One bad encounter resets your progress | Hold a standing metal buffer |
| Skipping refinement | Common metal can't cover advanced builds | Build refining capacity before tier-two tech |

If you only fix two of these, fix storage caps and the crisis reserve. Together they turn metal collection from a scramble into a buffer you can actually plan around.

## FAQ

**What is the fastest way to improve metal collection in Star Trek Outposts Unknown?**
Upgrade the extractor you already have before building a second one, then make sure your storage can hold the extra output. Adding capacity you cannot store simply wastes power and crew time.

**Do I need to refine metal, or can I build with raw material?**
Raw common metal covers basic construction. Advanced facilities and higher-tier tech generally expect processed material, so refining capacity should be built before you chase tier-two research — not after you discover you need it.

**Should I stockpile metal or spend it immediately?**
Hold a standing reserve sized to rebuild your most important module, and spend the surplus. A successful Star Trek Outposts Unknown metal collection strategy is a buffer plus a spend plan, never one or the other.

**Does metal collection affect the story?**
Indirectly, yes. Narrative missions in the X'Lehari System assume a functioning outpost. Falling behind on construction delays the facilities you need to render aid — so efficient metal collection is what keeps the story moving at your pace rather than the game's.
